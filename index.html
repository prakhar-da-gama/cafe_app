Our Story · 우리 이야기

Homesick for Seoul, two steps from the stupa.

In 2016, Ananya Rai came home to Sarnath after six years of student winters in Seoul — nights spent in the little cafes of Ikseon-dong and Hongdae, nursing a hot yuja-cha while the snow fell. She missed the warmth of those rooms more than she expected.

So she rented a quiet two-room house a short walk from the Dhamek Stupa, hung a hand-painted sign reading Seoulmate Cafe (서울메이트), and set out to recreate that feeling: dalgona whisked by hand, hotteok pressed on a hot griddle, bingsu shaved to order. Pilgrims, students and curious neighbours wandered in — and Sarnath quietly fell in love with Seoul.

Meet the Chef · 셰프 소개

Seoul taught her; Sarnath grounds her.

Ananya learned to cook in the back kitchens of Hongdae and the market stalls of Gwangjang, where an ajumma taught her to press hotteok until the sugar sang. She came home carrying recipes, a gochujang obsession, and a longing to share it all.

“Seoul felt like a soulmate the moment I arrived. This cafe is my way of introducing the two cities I love — one cup, one plate, one afternoon at a time.”

Her kitchen keeps it honest: gochujang simmered slow, soybean powder roasted in-house, citron steeped for weeks. Everything made the Seoul way — warm, generous, and rooted here by the Ganges.
